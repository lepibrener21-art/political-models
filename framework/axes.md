# Evaluation axes

Each model is scored 1 (very low) to 5 (very high) on six axes. The first three describe what a system gives people; the last three describe whether it can actually work.

| Key | Axis | Question |
|---|---|---|
| F | Personal freedom | How much of each person's life stays free from interference, including by majorities? |
| P | Popular power | How much real say do ordinary people have over collective decisions? |
| S | Protection | Security, rule of law, and rights that hold even against the powerful or the majority. |
| C | Scale | Does it work for millions of strangers, not just a village? |
| R | Resilience | Can it resist capture, collapse, or conquest over time? |
| E | Evidence | How well tested is it? 1 = theory only, 3 = local trials, 5 = national-scale track record. |

## Rubric

- **1** absent or actively undermined
- **2** weak, easily overridden
- **3** moderate, depends heavily on surrounding institutions
- **4** strong under normal conditions
- **5** strong and robust under stress

## Notes

- F and P echo Constant's "liberty of the moderns" vs "of the ancients" [constant1819] and Berlin's negative vs positive liberty [berlin1958].
- F and P can conflict: a powerful majority can restrict individual freedom (see `baselines.md`, direct democracy).
- Low S tends to drag F down in practice: freedom without protection becomes whatever the strongest local actor allows.
- Many models are **add-ons** to an existing system, not replacements. Score them as they would work inside a constitutional democracy unless the card says otherwise.
