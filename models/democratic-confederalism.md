# Democratic confederalism / libertarian municipalism

**In one line:** stateless self-government through neighborhood assemblies federated upward by recallable delegates.

## Mechanism
- Base unit: face-to-face assembly of a neighborhood or village.
- Assemblies send mandated, recallable delegates to councils at higher levels.
- Commitments to gender equality (co-chairs, quotas), ecology, and minority inclusion.

## Origins
- Murray Bookchin's libertarian municipalism [bookchin2015].
- Adapted by Abdullah Öcalan as "democratic confederalism".

## Real-world use
- North and East Syria (Rojava), from about 2012, during the civil war. Its status since the 2024 change of government in Syria is uncertain; check current news.

## Strengths
- Very high direct participation; strong gender equality provisions.
- Shows a stateless-inspired model can run a large region under pressure.

## Weaknesses
- Wartime conditions and a dominant party/militia blur the line between ideals and practice; critics report restrictions on rivals and conscription.
- Survival depends on external military protection.
- Untested in peacetime at national scale.

## Open questions
- Can the assembly model persist once integrated into, or negotiated with, a central state?
