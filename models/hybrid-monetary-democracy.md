# Hybrid monetary democracy

**In one line:** a protected, tax-funded core budget plus an equal civic budget per resident that each person directs to public projects.

## Mechanism
- Core budget: compulsory tax funds courts, police, audit, debt, the safety net and maintenance; its scope changes only by constitutional process.
- Directed share: a fixed share of revenue is split into equal civic budgets per resident, regardless of income or tax paid.
- Residents allocate it across projects vetted by an independent agency.
- Optional private top-ups get quadratic matching from a public pool.
- All-or-nothing targets; funds released at milestones; budgets must cover lifetime running costs.
- Unallocated budgets follow a default set by the elected council, or a revocable delegate (see `liquid-democracy`).
- Projects expire unless refunded; audits are paid from the core.

## Origins
- Developed here as a fix for `direct-monetary-democracy`.
- Combines participatory budgeting [baiocchi2005], equal public vouchers [ackerman2002], quadratic funding [buterin2019], assurance contracts [tabarrok1998] and revocable delegation [ford2002].

## Real-world use
- Not tried as a whole.
- Parts are tested: `participatory-budgeting` in thousands of cities; Seattle democracy vouchers, equal public money per resident for campaigns (first used 2017); Gitcoin's quadratic funding of open-source grants.
- Hungary's 1% law and Italy's *5 per mille* let taxpayers direct a slice of tax, but in proportion to tax paid, not equally.

## Strengths
- Keeps one person, one vote: everyone directs the same amount.
- Courts, rights and maintenance stay out of popularity contests.
- Concrete say over a real share of spending, with fast accountability.
- Can start small and grow the directed share step by step.

## Weaknesses
- The core/directed boundary is where power moves: whoever defines "core" controls most money, so the directed share may stay symbolic, or, if large, starve coordination.
- Organized groups can dominate allocation while most residents use the default.
- Quadratic matching is vulnerable to collusion and fake identities (see `quadratic-voting`).
- Directed projects create running costs that can land on the core.
- More complex than taxes or voting alone.

## Open questions
- What directed share is workable before quality suffers?
- Should residents split budgets freely (shows intensity) or vote on a slate (simpler, harder to game)?
- Does the default keep elected officials in control by the back door?
- Can identity checks make matching secure at national scale without harming privacy?
