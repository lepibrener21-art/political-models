# Liquid democracy

**In one line:** on every issue, vote yourself or delegate your vote to someone you trust, and take it back at any time.

## Mechanism
- Direct vote on any issue, or delegate.
- Delegation can be per topic (health to one person, economy to another).
- Delegations are revocable instantly, even right before a vote.
- Transitive: a delegate can pass votes on, forming chains.

## Origins
- Proxy-voting sketch by Charles Dodgson (Lewis Carroll), 1884 [dodgson1884].
- Similar proposal by James Miller, 1969 [miller1969].
- Modern form: Bryan Ford, "Delegative Democracy", 2002 [ford2002].

## Real-world use
- German Pirate Party, ~2010–2013, via LiquidFeedback software. Votes concentrated in a few delegates.
- Demoex, Vallentuna (Sweden), from 2002: councillor followed members' online votes.
- DemocracyOS / Partido de la Red (Argentina); Google Votes internal trial (~2015); Flux party (Australia).
- Delegated token voting in many blockchain DAOs.

## Strengths
- Bridges direct and representative democracy; avoids voter fatigue.
- Rewards expertise without a fixed elite.
- Continuous accountability through revocation.

## Weaknesses
- Super-voters: delegation concentrates power; may reduce decision accuracy in some models [kahng2018].
- Conflict between transparency (needed for accountability) and the secret ballot (needed against coercion and vote-buying).
- Active, tech-savvy users dominate; forgotten delegations drift.
- Digital security and trust in software at national scale.
- Does not by itself protect minorities; needs constitutional limits.

## Open questions
- Can caps on delegated votes per person prevent concentration without killing the benefit?
- Can delegations be private while delegates' votes are public?
