# Participatory budgeting

**In one line:** residents directly propose and vote on how part of a public budget is spent.

## Mechanism
- A share of the budget is set aside.
- Residents propose projects in neighborhood meetings or online.
- Proposals are checked for feasibility, then put to a public vote.
- Winning projects are funded and tracked.

## Origins
- Porto Alegre, Brazil, 1989, under the Workers' Party [baiocchi2005].

## Real-world use
- Spread to thousands of cities; notable cases include Paris (a share of the investment budget), New York, and many European municipalities.
- Porto Alegre studies link it to more spending on sanitation and health in poor districts [baiocchi2005].

## Strengths
- Concrete, visible results build trust.
- Brings in people who rarely vote.
- Easy to start small and expand.

## Weaknesses
- Usually controls only a small share of the budget; can be symbolic.
- Depends on political will; Porto Alegre's version weakened after changes in city government.
- Organized groups can dominate turnout.

## Open questions
- What share of a budget can be handed over before quality or coordination suffers?
