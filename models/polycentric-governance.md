# Polycentric governance and subsidiarity

**In one line:** many overlapping centers of decision-making, each handling what it can do best, with problems solved at the lowest workable level.

## Mechanism
- Local, regional, national and functional bodies (e.g. water boards) share authority.
- Subsidiarity: a higher level acts only when a lower level cannot.
- Communities can create their own rules for shared resources.

## Origins
- Vincent and Elinor Ostrom; Elinor Ostrom's eight design principles for managing commons [ostrom1990]. Nobel Prize in economics, 2009.
- Subsidiarity appears in Catholic social teaching and the EU treaties.

## Real-world use
- Swiss federalism: strong cantons and municipalities.
- Community-run irrigation, fisheries and forests studied by Ostrom worldwide.

## Strengths
- Decisions closer to the people affected; easier to experiment and learn from other units.
- No single point of failure or capture.
- Proven at national scale in federal countries.

## Weaknesses
- Complex and sometimes slow; unclear who is responsible.
- Rich regions can pull ahead of poor ones.
- Local majorities can oppress local minorities without a higher level stepping in.

## Open questions
- Which issues truly need a global level (climate, pandemics, AI)?
